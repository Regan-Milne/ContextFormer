# Mapped KV: Write-Time Memory Geography for Long-Context Decoding on Frozen LLMs

**Author:** Regan Milne (github.com/Regan-Milne)
**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Status:** Preprint / technical report. Code release forthcoming (see §8). This
document is a timestamped disclosure of the mechanism and results.
**License (this document & the forthcoming code):** Apache-2.0.

---

## Abstract

Autoregressive LLM decoding on local hardware is increasingly bounded by the
KV cache: its memory-bandwidth cost per token and its VRAM residency both grow
with context, capping usable context length and dominating decode energy. We
present **mapped KV**, a training-free modification to a **frozen** pretrained
model's attention. A recent dense window plus the attention-sink prefix are
kept verbatim; as tokens age out of the window their keys/values are **placed
at write time** into content-addressed regions. At each decode step the query
is **routed** to a few regions and attention runs only over the selected
neighborhoods plus the window and sinks. Unlike eviction (H2O, SnapKV) or
sink+window truncation (StreamingLLM) nothing is discarded; and unlike
clustered-KV retrieval systems that estimate the unretrieved tail to preserve
full-attention accuracy - closest among them **RetroInfer** (Lu et al., 2025)
 -  mapped KV runs **exact** attention over only its selected regions with **no
tail estimation**, and is demonstrated on a **frozen hybrid** (Gated-DeltaNet
+ attention) model. We meter success in **KV entries touched per token**, the
quantity that sets local-AI bandwidth and energy.

The mechanism was frozen on a 0.5B model and carried **without
architecture-specific retuning** through 3B, the Qwen3.5 hybrid
(Gated-DeltaNet + attention) family at 2B/4B, and a 9B production target.
Under an adversarial, pre-registered protocol it holds near-full quality
(ΔPPL +1.4 / +3.8 / +7.5% at 8k / 16k / 32k on Qwen3.5-2B) with intact far
retrieval, while the per-attention-layer KV touch advantage **grows** with
context (6.1× → 15.7×); random-routing and window-only controls collapse
(ΔPPL up to +331%, 0/6 passkey). Built into a real incremental-decode runtime,
mapped KV cuts physical KV traffic 16–31× at 32k and reduces decode energy up
to ~2× net J/token versus stock attention, growing with context. A
region-granular **RAM↔VRAM tier** then lets cold KV leave VRAM: on a 24 GB
card a 9B runs 32k and 64k contexts that do **not** fit resident - logical
context exceeds physical KV residency by ≥2×, retrieval intact.

We also report, honestly, where the mechanism does **not** win: against a
heavily-optimized full-attention baseline (llama.cpp with flash attention and
KV-quantization) at mainstream context lengths, we are slower, and that
baseline already fits a 27B at 128k on 24 GB. The differentiated, robust value
of mapped KV is **capacity beyond VRAM** (context tiered to host RAM, past what
VRAM can hold even with KV-quant) and **flat decode/energy cost at extreme
depth** - the long-memory-agent regime.

## 1. Introduction

On local hardware, the KV cache - not the weights - is the scaling wall for
long context. Weights are a fixed cost that quantizes well; the KV cache grows
linearly with context in both the bandwidth read per generated token and the
VRAM it occupies. As weights are quantized to fit larger models on consumer
GPUs, the KV cache's *share* of decode cost rises.

Our thesis: **a frozen pretrained model already contains a navigable memory
geography** - its keys are organized enough that a query can be routed to a
small set of relevant regions instead of attending over the entire history -
and this can be exploited at inference time with **no fine-tuning**.

**Contributions.**
1. A training-free mechanism that organizes a frozen model's KV cache into
   content-addressed regions **at write time** and routes each decode step to a
   few regions.
2. A **touch/traffic-first** evaluation methodology (KV entries touched per
   token), appropriate to local-AI economics.
3. An **adversarial, pre-registered validation ladder** with controls.
4. **Transfer** across scale (0.5B→3B→9B) and architecture family (dense →
   Qwen3.5 hybrid) with zero architecture-specific retuning.
5. A runtime with **RAM↔VRAM tiering** that provides context beyond VRAM, with
   measured energy, traffic, and capacity.

## 2. Related work and differentiators

KV-compression methods fall into: **eviction** (H2O, SnapKV - discard
low-value entries), **sink+window** (StreamingLLM - keep the prefix and a
recent window, forget the middle), and **retrieval over a clustered/ANN KV
store** (InfLLM, RetrievalAttention, ClusterKV, and - closest to this work -
**RetroInfer**).

**Nearest neighbor: RetroInfer** (Lu et al., 2025; arXiv:2505.02922). Like
mapped KV, it treats the KV cache as a clustered vector store: it clusters
keys, keeps centroids as a small index, scores the query against those
centroids, retrieves only the selected clusters, and tiers cold KV to host RAM
with incremental index updates. On dense-attention models it scales this to
120K–1M-token contexts with custom GPU/CPU kernels and large throughput gains
(up to 4.4× over full attention at 120K, 12.2× over sparse baselines at 1M) at
full-attention-level accuracy. We do **not** claim the cluster–route–tier
skeleton as novel: RetroInfer establishes it and scales it beyond what we
demonstrate. And because RetroInfer's index is itself updated incrementally
during decoding, the "write-time vs. post-hoc index" distinction does not
separate the two systems, and we do not rest on it.

Mapped KV differs from RetroInfer on three axes we can state precisely:

1. **No attention estimation.** RetroInfer's central device is an
   accuracy-bounded *estimation zone*: unretrieved clusters still contribute
   an approximated attention mass computed from their centroids, which is how
   it preserves full-attention accuracy. Mapped KV runs **exact** softmax over
   only {sinks ∪ window ∪ top-*r* selected regions}; unselected regions
   contribute nothing. This is strictly simpler and more aggressive, and it
   costs some perplexity (ΔPPL +1.4 / +3.8 / +7.5% at 8k / 16k / 32k on
   Qwen3.5-2B) - yet discrete far-retrieval survives (passkey held). The
   finding of interest is therefore that, **for retrieval, the estimation
   machinery may be unnecessary**: hard top-*r* selection suffices when a
   small perplexity cost is acceptable.
2. **Frozen hybrid target.** RetroInfer is demonstrated on dense-attention
   transformers (Llama-3, Qwen2.5, DeepSeek-R1-Distill). Mapped KV is grafted
   onto a **frozen Qwen3.5 hybrid** (Gated-DeltaNet + attention) with zero
   architecture-specific retuning, routing only the small subset of
   conventional-attention layers - which appear to carry a disproportionate
   long-range, content-addressable role.
3. **Touch-first, pre-registered, adversarial evaluation.** We meter **KV
   entries touched per token** (the quantity that sets local-AI bandwidth and
   energy) rather than headline throughput, and every phase is pre-registered
   with window-only and random-routing controls that must collapse (they do:
   0/6 passkey, ΔPPL up to +331%).

Relative to eviction (H2O, SnapKV) and sink+window (StreamingLLM), mapped KV
also **evicts nothing** - all context is retained and tiered, not discarded.

## 3. The mapped-KV mechanism

**Store layout.** Per conventional-attention layer, the KV store is: the
attention-**sink** prefix (first N_SINK positions) + a recent dense **window**
(WINDOW positions), both kept verbatim; plus **aged regions** - older KV
grouped by content.

**Write-time placement.** As a token ages out of the dense window, its key/value
is assigned to a region via running, k-means-style centroids maintained per
layer and per KV-head group. Organization happens once, at write time.

**Per-token routing.** At each decode step, the query scores the centroids,
selects the top-*r* regions, and attention runs only over {window ∪ sinks ∪
selected regions}. Breadth *r* is a user-visible knob (r8 favors discrete
needle retrieval; r16 favors perplexity).

**Two frozen variants.** *Fixed-centroid* (centroids frozen after warm-up) and
*B1* (periodic wholesale re-clustering of accumulated content, budgeted like
garbage collection). Both travel unchanged across models; neither is crowned.

**RAM↔VRAM tiering.** The hot set (window + sinks + centroids + a small LRU of
recently-selected regions) lives in VRAM; cold aged regions live in **pinned
host RAM** and are gathered over PCIe on selection. Selection semantics are
unchanged - routing decides *which* regions; tiering only changes *where* their
bytes live.

## 4. Validation methodology

Every phase was **pre-registered** before its build, with interpretations fixed
in advance and success rates reported only at pre-registered seed counts.
Probes are **adversarial**: fresh long documents, out-of-distribution domains,
and needle/passkey retrieval that a window-only baseline provably fails.
**Controls**: window-only (the forgetting baseline) and random-region routing
(routing must beat luck). Every implementation optimization was gated for
**bit-honest equivalence** - identical touched-sets and NLL within the original
tolerance - re-run after each change; two real bugs were caught this way.

## 5. Results - teacher-forced ladder

**5.1 Geography exists in a frozen model.** Probes on a frozen 0.5B model show
its co-relevant keys are navigable: a query concentrates on a small set of
regions well above chance.

**5.2 Training-free graft; the advantage grows with context.** The training-free
graft holds near-full quality while touching a small fraction of the cache, and
the **touch advantage grows with context length** - the make-or-break scaling
signal. A learned-router variant was tested and **rejected** at the transfer
gate; training-free routing wins.

**5.3 Transfer across scale and family, zero retuning.** The mechanism, frozen
on 0.5B, was carried unchanged to 3B and then to the **Qwen3.5 hybrid**
(Gated-DeltaNet + attention) at 2B, and reproduced at 4B. Headline transfer
result (Qwen3.5-2B, B1-r16):

| ctx | ΔPPL | touch% | per-attn-layer advantage | passkey (full / b1-r16) |
|----:|-----:|-------:|-------------------------:|:-----------------------:|
| 8k  | +1.4% | 16.5 | 6.1× | 6/6 / 6/6 |
| 16k | +3.8% | 9.5  | 10.5× | 6/6 / 5/6 |
| 32k | +7.5% | 6.4  | 15.7× | 6/6 / 5/6 |

Controls collapse: random-routing +88.6%→+237% ΔPPL and window-only
+160%→+331% across 8k→32k, both 0/6 passkey at every length. The six
KV-attention layers of the hybrid appear to carry a disproportionately
important long-range, content-addressable role.

## 6. Results - the runtime (generation-mode, metered)

**R0 - decode core.** An incremental-decode engine on the frozen Qwen3.5-2B
passes decode-equivalence and mapped-equivalence gates and scores **54/54** on
generation-mode passkey across contexts and configs.

**R9 - production target.** The frozen mechanism instantiates and behaves
correctly on Qwen3.5-**9B**, unchanged (perfect passkey at 8k/16k), confirming
transfer to the deployment target.

**R1 - energy and traffic (2B, at the wall).** Paired runs, CUDA-synchronized,
nvidia-smi power. Stock full attention's power grows with cache size while
mapped stays near-flat; net energy advantage **1.48× @8k → 1.60× @16k → 1.83×
@32k** (b1-r16; 2.06× b1-r8). Physical KV traffic at 32k: b1-r16 reads 2,022 of
32,768 positions (**16.2× less**; b1-r8 **31×**). The like-for-like whole-model
gain (b1-r16 vs the same engine's full attention at 32k) is **1.10×**, matching
an a-priori Amdahl bound of ~1.09× - the accounting validated end to end.

**R2 - capacity beyond VRAM.** With cold KV tiered to pinned host RAM, the 9B
runs **32k and 64k** contexts that do **not** fit resident bf16 on a 24 GB card
(3/3 passkey at both), peaks 20.1 / 21.4 GB, KV tiered out 1.05 / 2.09 GB.
**Logical context exceeds physical KV residency by ≥2×**, retrieval intact.

**R2.1 - latency paydown.** Batched/de-serialized cold-fetch path: cold-storm
p90 207 → 134 ms; 32k decode ~112–127 ms/tok (from ~156–190). The hot cache
becomes a *traffic* knob rather than a latency knob - ~87 MB captures nearly all
the latency.

## 7. Honest positioning and limits

We measured the mechanism against a heavily-optimized full-attention baseline
and against its own implementation, and report both plainly.

**7.1 Batch-1 decode is dominated by non-matmul overhead.** Profiling the
mapped decode at batch 1 shows the quantizable weight-matmul is a *minority* of
per-token time; a large eager-mode / kernel-launch overhead ("the waiter")
dominates, with the GPU idle much of each step. The primary latency lever is
therefore CUDA graphs, not the attention mechanism.

**7.2 Optimized full attention is fast and holds to 128k.** Baseline:
Qwen3.8-27B (Unsloth UD-Q5_K_S GGUF) in llama.cpp on a 24 GB RTX 4090, flash
attention + q8_0 KV-quant:

| context depth | decode tok/s |
|---:|---:|
| 0    | 39.9 |
| 8k   | 39.7 |
| 32k  | 37.7 |
| 65k  | 34.0 |
| 128k | 28.7 |

128k fits 24 GB comfortably; with heavier KV-quant the ceiling reaches
~200–400k. The full-attention "sag" with depth is real and accelerating
(15% → 28% by 128k) but bites from a fast baseline.

**7.3 Where mapped KV wins, honestly.** It does **not** beat optimized full
attention on speed at mainstream context, and quality parity ("on par") is the
target, not a claimed advantage. The differentiated, robust value is:
- **Capacity beyond VRAM (unconditional):** cold KV lives in host RAM, so
  usable context is RAM-bounded, not VRAM-bounded - an order of magnitude past
  what an optimized full-attention stack can hold on the same card even with
  KV-quant. We run what they cannot.
- **Flat decode/energy at extreme depth (conditional):** routing keeps
  per-token KV cost ~flat while full attention's KV read eventually rivals the
  weight read; realized in wall-clock once the eager floor is removed.

The natural regime for this is **long-memory agents** - continuous sessions
that accumulate very large, growing context.

**7.4 Limits.** PyTorch/NVIDIA runtime (a llama.cpp port is future work); the
eager-mode floor above; the extreme-context niche; and quantization is a
capacity means, not a speed win at batch 1.
